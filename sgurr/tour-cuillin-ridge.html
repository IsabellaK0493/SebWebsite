Sgùrr nan Gillean, Cuillin Ridge

The peak our name comes from. A three-day expedition onto the gabbro of the Black Cuillin, roped scrambling on rock that grips like sandpaper and drops that don't forgive a slip.

Why we named the company after this ridge

Sgùrr nan Gillean — "peak of the young men" — is the sentinel of the Cuillin ridge as seen from Sligachan, and the introduction most climbers get to Skye's gabbro rock. It's not the hardest summit on the ridge, but the approach and the final Pinnacle Ridge scramble give an honest sample of everything the Cuillin asks of you: route-finding on featureless rock, comfort with sustained exposure, and trust in your rope work.

Grid reference NG 471 253. The rock here is gabbro — coarse, grippy, and notorious for destroying a compass bearing near its higher iron content. We navigate by terrain, not just instrument, on this ridge.

The expedition

Day 1

Skills day, Sligachan

Rope work, moving together on exposed ground, and a short scramble to check technique and pace before committing to the ridge proper.

Day 2

Sgùrr nan Gillean via Pinnacle Ridge

The main event: an early start onto the Pinnacle Ridge, a sustained, exposed scramble to the summit, and a careful descent via the West Ridge.

Day 3

Am Basteir or weather day

Conditions permitting, a second ridge summit; otherwise a lower-level day reviewing rope skills for a future return to the ridge.
