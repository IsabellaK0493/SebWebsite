Ben Nevis, via Càrn Mòr Dearg Arête

Beinn Nibheis — the highest ground in Britain, reached the long way: over Càrn Mòr Dearg and along a narrow, exposed arête with the mountain's north face dropping away beside you the whole time.

Why the arête, not the tourist path

Most people who climb Ben Nevis take the Mountain Track and see a summit plateau of shattered stone and cloud. The Càrn Mòr Dearg Arête is a different mountain entirely: a mile of narrow, blocky ridge with the sheer north face of Ben Nevis rising beside you, finishing with a boulder scramble onto the summit itself. It's not technical climbing, but it is a genuinely serious scramble, with no easy retreat once you're committed to the ridge.

Grid reference NN 166 712 marks the summit trig point and Ordnance Survey shelter — usually the busiest and coldest square metre in Scotland.

The route

06:30

Start, North Face car park

Early start from Fort William, walking in via the Allt a' Mhuilinn to the foot of Ben Nevis's north face.

09:00

Càrn Mòr Dearg summit

A steady ascent to the first summit of the day, with the full arête laid out ahead of you.

11:00

The arête

Roped or unroped depending on conditions and group confidence — a sustained, narrow crest with exposure on both sides.

13:30

Summit of Ben Nevis

A boulder-field scramble onto the plateau, and the trig point at the highest ground in Britain.

15:00

Descent via the Mountain Track

Down the standard tourist path, giving your legs a break from rough ground for the return to Fort William.
