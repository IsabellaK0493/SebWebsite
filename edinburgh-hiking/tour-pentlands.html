Regional Park

The Pentland Hills

Stretching across 56 square kilometres at the edge of Edinburgh, the Pentland Hills offer a vast landscape of rolling hills, woodlands and reservoirs. Despite being so close to the city centre, the park feels worlds apart and from the higher summits you get great views of the entire city.

Why the Pentlands

My first adventure to the Pentlands was over 20 years ago, when I was just 2 years old. Back then, I spent my time collecting tadpoles and splashing through muddy puddles with our family chocolate Labrador, Ginty.

Since those early days, my love for the Pentlands has only deepened through decades of walks and, in recent years, trail runs and I have navigated pretty much every part of the Pentlands. So much so that the hills feel like home to me and in fact, Robert Louis Stevenson famously called the Pentlands the 'Hills of Home' for that good reason.

The Pentlands offer a wide choice of trails, from gentle walks to more challenging routes with options that go over the many tops like Carnethy Hill, Scald Law, and the Kips, as well as routes that take you through forested areas or past reservoirs.

I look forward to guiding you through these breathtaking hills and sharing my knowledge of their history.

On the day

Start

Meet

We will meet at a trailhead for the Pentlands. Typically, for those doing their first tour, we will begin near the trailhead that is just off the A720. This can be accessed easily by using various Lothian buses (including the numbers 4, 5, 16, 18 and 27) and then taking a short walk.

1

Up to Caerketton

After meeting up, we will pass Swanston Farm, where the Highland cows are often visible, and then make our way to Caerketton peak, which will make up most of the ascent for the day.

2

Along to Allermuir

The short route between these two peaks offers 360 degree views across the whole of Edinburgh, the Firth of Forth, and much of the rest of the Pentland range.

3

Over to Castlelaw

This hill includes a well-preserved Iron Age hill fort and earth house. It is also the site of an active military firing range.

4

Past Glencorse Reservoir

One of the most iconic areas of the Pentlands, Glencorse Reservoir is an important water source for the local area.

5

Bonaly and Back

We will take a different line back to the start point, going past Bonaly Reservoir and through the forested area of Bonaly Country Park.

Example of a common route we take for the half day tour. Other routes available at request or for repeat tours. Routes will vary depending on weather and the group's pace and capabilities.
