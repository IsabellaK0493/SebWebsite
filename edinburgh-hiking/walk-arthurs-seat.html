Arthur's Seat & Salisbury Crags

The hill that gives Edinburgh its shape. I have walked and run this ground hundreds of times and know every path up it, including the ones most visitors never find.

An extinct volcano in the middle of a capital city

Having lived in Edinburgh for most of my life, Arthur's Seat and Salisbury Crags are very familiar to me. I have run hundreds of laps of this park, and explored just about every possible path and route through it.

Arthur's Seat is the remains of a volcano that was active around 340 million years ago. The Crags are the edge of a sill of harder rock — it was here that James Hutton found the evidence that led him to modern geology, which makes this a genuinely important few hundred metres of hillside.

For being so centrally located, it holds a good choice of routes. There are gentler ways around the back past St Anthony's Chapel, and more inclined lines that take you straight up onto the ridge.
